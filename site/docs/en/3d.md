---
title: 3D, games and physics
description: Draw 3D scenes in a canvas with active::scene, write games with active::ecs, and give every object a mass with active::physics, in Rust and WebGL 2, without JavaScript.
---

Active puts 3D in a page with nothing to add, all in the `active` crate:

- **`active::scene`**: a scene of meshes, lights and cameras, geometries, physically based materials, a WebGL 2 renderer, an animation loop and orbit controls.
- **`active::ecs`**: Active's game engine. Entities, components and systems, an `App` with plugins and schedules, states, messages, observers, and 3D in physical light units, drawn by `active::scene`.
- **`active::physics`**: mass, forces and collisions for every object of both, run by [Rapier](https://rapier.rs) and checked against the formulas of mechanics.

What it brings:

- **One language, one project.** The pages, the server, the 3D and the game are the same Rust: types shared from end to end, no JavaScript bundle to build, no glue between two worlds.
- **Nothing to add.** The renderer uses the WebGL 2 bindings of `web-sys`, already there with the `client` feature, and Cargo brings Rapier for the physics.
- **Tested without a browser.** Everything but drawing runs on the server and in `cargo test`: a game is a world you step and check, frame by frame.
- **Light and fast.** One shader for every material, a game loop with no allocation per frame, and physics that sleeps when nothing moves.

The 3D demo of the home page is an `active::ecs` game, with physics.

## A scene to draw: `active::scene`

A scene, a camera and a renderer. In a component, the canvas comes from `use_action`, and the loop stops when the element goes:

```rust
use active::prelude::*;
use active::scene::*;

fn cube(_props: &str) -> Node {
    canvas().class("cube").use_action(start).into()
}

/// In the browser: starts the scene, and gives what stops it when the element goes.
#[cfg(target_arch = "wasm32")]
fn start(canvas: &active::Dom) -> impl FnOnce() + use<> {
    let mut scene = Scene::new();
    let cube = scene.add(Mesh::new(
        BoxGeometry::new(1.0, 1.0, 1.0),
        MeshStandardMaterial::new().color(0x44aa88).roughness(0.4),
    ));
    scene.add(AmbientLight::new(0xffffff, 0.5));
    let sun = scene.add(DirectionalLight::new(0xffffff, 3.0));
    scene[sun].position.set(-1.0, 2.0, 4.0);

    let mut camera = PerspectiveCamera::new(60.0, 0.0, 0.1, 100.0); // aspect 0: the canvas's
    camera.position.set(0.0, 1.0, 3.0);
    let mut controls = OrbitControls::new(canvas);
    controls.enable_damping = true;

    let looping = WebGLRenderer::new(canvas).ok().map(|mut renderer| {
        renderer.set_pixel_ratio(device_pixel_ratio());
        animation_loop(move |time| {
            scene[cube].rotation.y = time as f32 / 1000.0;
            controls.update(&mut camera);
            renderer.render(&scene, &camera);
        })
    });
    move || drop(looping) // stops the loop
}

/// On the server: nothing to start.
#[cfg(not(target_arch = "wasm32"))]
fn start(_canvas: &active::Dom) -> impl FnOnce() + use<> {
    || {}
}
```

The renderer follows the size the page gives the canvas (style it with CSS), times the pixel ratio. Without a background (`scene.background = Some(..)`), the canvas is transparent.

| You want to | You write |
|---|---|
| Make a scene | `Scene::new()` |
| Add an object | `let id = scene.add(Mesh::new(geometry, material))` |
| Turn it | `scene[id].rotation.y += 0.01` |
| Group objects | `scene.add_to(group, child)` |
| A box of 1 m | `BoxGeometry::new(1.0, 1.0, 1.0)` |
| A colored material | `MeshStandardMaterial::new().color(0x44aa88)` |
| A light | `PointLight::new(0xffffff, 50.0)` |
| Aim the camera | `camera.look_at(Vector3::ZERO)` |
| Draw every frame | `let looping = animation_loop(f)` (stops when dropped) |
| Turn the camera with the mouse | `OrbitControls::new(canvas)`, then `controls.update(&mut camera)` |
| A filmic look | `renderer.tone_mapping = ToneMapping::AcesFilmic` |

What is there:

- **Maths**: `Vector2`, `Vector3`, `Euler`, `Quaternion`, `Matrix4`, `Color` (linear, from hexadecimal or sRGB), with their short names too (`Vec3`, `Quat`, `Mat4`).
- **Geometries**: `BoxGeometry`, `PlaneGeometry`, `SphereGeometry`, `CylinderGeometry`, `ConeGeometry`, `TorusGeometry`, and `BufferGeometry` from your own arrays.
- **Materials**: `MeshBasicMaterial`, `MeshLambertMaterial`, `MeshStandardMaterial` (roughness, metalness, emissive, flat shading) and `MeshNormalMaterial`, with `opacity`, `transparent`, `side` and `wireframe`.
- **Lights**: `AmbientLight`, `HemisphereLight`, `DirectionalLight` and `PointLight`, physically based (a directional light of intensity π is white on a white surface).
- **Cameras**: `PerspectiveCamera` and `OrthographicCamera`; `OrbitControls` with the mouse, a finger, the wheel or two fingers.

Not there yet: textures, shadows, loaders (glTF), spot lights and post-processing.

## A game: `active::ecs`

A disc, a cube, a light and a camera, plus a system that turns the cube.

```rust
use active::ecs::prelude::*;

struct Spinning;
active::component!(Spinning); // a component, declared in one line

fn setup(
    mut commands: Commands,
    mut meshes: ResMut<Assets<Mesh>>,
    mut materials: ResMut<Assets<StandardMaterial>>,
) {
    commands.spawn((
        Mesh3d(meshes.add(Circle::new(4.0))),
        MeshMaterial3d(materials.add(Color::WHITE)),
        Transform::from_rotation(Quat::from_rotation_x(-std::f32::consts::FRAC_PI_2)),
    ));
    commands.spawn((
        Mesh3d(meshes.add(Cuboid::new(1.0, 1.0, 1.0))),
        MeshMaterial3d(materials.add(Color::srgb_u8(124, 144, 255))),
        Transform::from_xyz(0.0, 0.5, 0.0),
        Spinning,
    ));
    commands.spawn((PointLight::default(), Transform::from_xyz(4.0, 8.0, 4.0)));
    commands.spawn((
        Camera3d::default(),
        Transform::from_xyz(-2.5, 4.5, 9.0).looking_at(Vec3::ZERO, Vec3::Y),
    ));
}

fn spin(time: Res<Time>, mut cubes: Query<&mut Transform, With<Spinning>>) {
    for mut transform in &mut cubes {
        transform.rotate_y(time.delta_secs());
    }
}

fn game() -> App {
    let mut app = App::new();
    app.add_plugins(DefaultPlugins)
        .add_systems(Startup, setup)
        .add_systems(Update, spin);
    app
}
```

In a component, run it in the canvas of an island and stop it when the element goes:

```rust
fn game_island(_props: &str) -> Node {
    canvas().class("game").use_action(play).into()
}

#[cfg(target_arch = "wasm32")]
fn play(canvas: &active::Dom) -> impl FnOnce() + use<> {
    let running = game().run_in(canvas).ok();
    move || drop(running) // stops the game
}

#[cfg(not(target_arch = "wasm32"))]
fn play(_canvas: &active::Dom) -> impl FnOnce() + use<> {
    || {}
}
```

For a page that is only the game, `game().run()` draws in the canvas named by `Window::canvas` (a CSS selector, set with `DefaultPlugins.set(WindowPlugin { .. })`), or in a canvas it adds to the page. The canvas takes the keyboard once clicked, so arrow keys do not scroll the page otherwise.

What is there:

- **ECS**: `World`, `Entity`, components and resources, bundles as tuples, `Query` with `&T`, `&mut T`, `Entity`, `Option`, `Has` and the filters `With`, `Without`, `Changed`, `Added` and `Or`; `Single`, `Res`, `ResMut`, `Local`, `Commands` (`spawn`, `insert`, `remove`, `despawn`, `with_children`), exclusive systems taking `&mut World`, and the required components (a `Transform` brings its `GlobalTransform`).
- **App**: plugins and plugin groups (`DefaultPlugins`, `MinimalPlugins`), the schedules `Startup`, `Update`, `FixedUpdate` (64 times a second), `PostUpdate` and the others, `run_if` with `in_state`, `resource_exists`, `on_timer`, `input_just_pressed`, `run_once` or your own function, and `chain`.
- **States, messages, observers**: `init_state`, `NextState`, `OnEnter`, `OnExit`; `add_message`, `MessageWriter`, `MessageReader`; `Event`, `commands.trigger` and `add_observer` with `On<E>`.
- **Time and input**: `Time` (and `Time<Fixed>` in `FixedUpdate`), `Timer`, `ButtonInput<KeyCode>` (keys by position, like `KeyboardEvent.code`), `ButtonInput<MouseButton>`, `AccumulatedMouseMotion`, `AccumulatedMouseScroll` and `Window::cursor_position`.
- **3D**: `Transform` and the hierarchy (`ChildOf`, `Children`, `Visibility`), `Assets` and `Handle`, the primitives `Cuboid`, `Sphere`, `Plane3d`, `Circle`, `Rectangle`, `Cylinder`, `Cone` and `Torus`, `StandardMaterial`, `Color` (sRGB, linear, HSL, `palettes::css`), `PointLight` in lumens, `DirectionalLight` in lux, `GlobalAmbientLight`, `Camera3d` with `Projection`, `Exposure` (EV100 9.7) and `Tonemapping`, and `Camera::viewport_to_world` to find what the pointer is over.

Good to know:

- Components, resources, messages, events and states are declared with `impl Component for Player {}`, or the macros `active::component!`, `resource!`, `message!`, `event!` and `states!`, in one line.
- The systems of a schedule run one after the other, in the order they were added: a game gives the same result every time. `.chain()` is accepted and always holds.
- Each component sits in its own cell: two queries may read the same component, and one may change it while another reads others. Reaching the same component of the same entity twice at the same time (`Query<&mut Transform>` and `Query<&Transform>` over the same entities) panics with a message naming the component, separate them with `With`/`Without`.
- Every tone mapping is drawn with ACES. There are no shadows, textures, spot lights, 2D, UI or audio.

## Physics: mass, forces and collisions

Every 3D object has a mass and physics around it, with nothing to add. In `active::ecs`, `DefaultPlugins` includes `PhysicsPlugin`: every entity with a `Mesh3d` gets a body. Its mass comes from its volume (density 1 kg/m³ by default; `ColliderDensity(1000.0)` is water), and it collides as its own shape: ball, box, cylinder, cone, frustum, ring (with its hole), and flat shapes as a slab under their surface.

- A mesh with a volume is `Dynamic`: gravity pulls it, it bounces, slides, rolls, tips over and stacks.
- A flat one (`Plane3d`, `Circle`, `Rectangle`) is `Static`: it is the ground.
- A child of another entity is `Kinematic`: it follows its parent and pushes what it meets.

`RigidBody` changes that choice. The other components: `Mass`, `ColliderDensity`, `LinearVelocity`, `AngularVelocity`, `ExternalForce` (kept, or applied once), `ExternalTorque`, `ExternalImpulse` (once), `Restitution`, `Friction`, `RollingFriction`, `GravityScale`, `LinearDamping`, `AngularDamping`, and `Collider` for a shape other than the mesh's. `Sleeping` marks the bodies that rest. The `Gravity` resource sets gravity, and the `Attraction` resource makes the bodies attract each other. The messages `CollisionStarted` and `CollisionEnded` say who touches whom.

```rust
use active::ecs::prelude::*;

struct Crate;
active::component!(Crate);

fn setup(mut commands: Commands, mut meshes: ResMut<Assets<Mesh>>, mut materials: ResMut<Assets<StandardMaterial>>) {
    // The ground: flat, so it stays where it is.
    commands.spawn((
        Mesh3d(meshes.add(Plane3d::default().mesh().size(20.0, 20.0))),
        MeshMaterial3d(materials.add(Color::srgb(0.3, 0.5, 0.3))),
    ));
    // A crate of 20 kg, thrown sideways, and a bouncy ball.
    commands.spawn((
        Mesh3d(meshes.add(Cuboid::new(1.0, 1.0, 1.0))),
        MeshMaterial3d(materials.add(Color::srgb_u8(124, 144, 255))),
        Transform::from_xyz(0.0, 4.0, 0.0),
        Mass(20.0),
        LinearVelocity(Vec3::new(2.0, 0.0, 0.0)),
        Crate,
    ));
    commands.spawn((
        Mesh3d(meshes.add(Sphere::new(0.5))),
        MeshMaterial3d(materials.add(Color::WHITE)),
        Transform::from_xyz(3.0, 6.0, 0.0),
        Restitution(0.9),
    ));
    commands.spawn((PointLight::default(), Transform::from_xyz(4.0, 8.0, 4.0)));
    commands.spawn((Camera3d::default(), Transform::from_xyz(-4.0, 5.0, 10.0).looking_at(Vec3::ZERO, Vec3::Y)));
}

/// Space makes the crates jump.
fn jump(keys: Res<ButtonInput<KeyCode>>, mut crates: Query<&mut LinearVelocity, With<Crate>>) {
    if keys.just_pressed(KeyCode::Space) {
        for mut velocity in &mut crates {
            velocity.y = 6.0;
        }
    }
}

/// Each landing is a message: a sound, a score, a spark…
fn landings(mut collisions: MessageReader<CollisionStarted>) {
    for CollisionStarted(a, b) in collisions.read() {
        let _ = (a, b); // the two entities that touched
    }
}
```

With `active::scene`, a `Physics` world does the same for a scene. Every mesh gets a body, and the scene follows the bodies:

```rust
let mut physics = Physics::new();
let looping = animation_loop(move |_time| {
    physics.step(&mut scene, 1.0 / 60.0); // the meshes follow their bodies
    renderer.render(&scene, &camera);
});
// A kick, a force, a mass, a static body:
physics.body_mut(ball).unwrap().apply_impulse(Vector3::new(0.0, 5.0, 0.0));
```

And `active::physics` is the engine on its own, without a scene: `PhysicsWorld`, `Body`, `Collider`. Units are SI: meters, kilograms, seconds, newtons.

How it works. The physics is [Rapier](https://rapier.rs), the Rust physics engine of Dimforge, also used by Bevy and in the browser as rapier.js. `Body` and `Collider` stay plain values in the types of `active::scene`: you read and change their fields between two steps, and `PhysicsWorld` keeps a Rapier body for each. Every step:

1. What your code changed since the last step (a place, a velocity, a shape, a mass, a material, a force) goes to Rapier, and wakes the body up.
2. The step is cut into steps of at most a sixtieth of a second (so the motion does not depend on the frame rate), each solved by Rapier in four substeps: broad phase, contacts, Coulomb friction, restitution, soft contacts so that stacks stand still, continuous collision detection (nothing goes through a thin wall), gyroscopic forces, and islands of bodies that fall asleep together and cost nothing until something touches them.
3. Active adds what Rapier does not have: mutual attraction (a Barnes–Hut octree, applied half before and half after the move, as leapfrog does, so orbits stay closed), rolling resistance, and, for the bodies far from anything, the exact parabola and the exact free rotation around the principal axes, which keeps the angular momentum of a tumbling body: it wobbles, and a spin around its middle axis flips over, as a real one does.
4. The bodies are read back, with the pairs that started or stopped touching.

The masses and inertias are the exact ones of each shape, including the rings (which collide as 24 capsules along their tube, so their hole is open) and the cut cones (a convex hull). `PhysicsWorld::rapier_mut` gives Rapier itself, for what `active::physics` does not wrap: joints, ray casts, collision groups.

The tests check the engine against the formulas: a thrown ball follows its parabola to a tenth of a millimeter, an elastic ball bounces back to the same height again and again, a box holds on a slope up to atan(μ) and slides beyond it at g (sin θ − μ cos θ), a ball rolls down at 5/7 g sin θ and a cylinder at 2/3 g sin θ, friction stops a box after v²/(2 μ g) whatever the direction, equal balls exchange their velocities, a tumbling brick keeps its angular momentum and energy, two bodies orbit in a circle, nothing goes through a 2 cm wall at 120 m/s, and a stack of twelve boxes stands and falls asleep.

## Testing

The ECS runs without a browser: build the app, call `app.update()`, and look at the world. `TimeUpdateStrategy::ManualDuration` makes time move by a fixed step, and `RenderFrame::extract` lists what the camera would draw:

```rust
let mut app = game();
app.insert_resource(TimeUpdateStrategy::ManualDuration(std::time::Duration::from_millis(500)));
app.update();
app.update();
let world = app.world();
let turned = world.query_filtered::<&Transform, With<Spinning>>().single(world).unwrap();
assert!(turned.rotation != Quat::IDENTITY);
let frame = RenderFrame::extract(world, Vec2::new(800.0, 600.0)).unwrap();
assert_eq!(frame.mesh_count(), 2);
```
